EXPERIMENT B - active attacker flips one bit in the ciphertext
==========================================================================
original byte[20] = 0x35
flipped  byte[20] = 0x34  (one bit changed)

Bob raises InvalidTag -> message rejected, never shown to user.
RESULT: integrity holds. GCM's tag covers the ciphertext, so any
        modification is detected. Note this is exactly what plain
        AES-CTR or AES-CBC would NOT give me - encryption alone is
        not integrity.

==========================================================================
EXPERIMENT B2 - attacker renumbers the record (attacks the counter)
==========================================================================
counter in header = 0
attacker rewrites counter to 9

RESULT: rejected. The counter is passed as AAD, so it is
        authenticated even though it travels in clear. If I had
        left it unauthenticated an attacker could reorder or
        suppress messages silently.

==========================================================================
EXPERIMENT C - attacker captures a record and re-sends it
==========================================================================
Bob first delivery : 'unlock the front door'  (accepted)
Bob second delivery: rejected - replay/reorder detected (counter 0)

RESULT: replay blocked by the monotonic counter. Worth noting the
        AEAD tag alone would NOT have caught this - a replayed
        record is perfectly authentic. Replay defence is protocol
        level state, not a property of the cipher.

==========================================================================
EXPERIMENT D - active MITM on an UNAUTHENTICATED handshake
==========================================================================
Mallory sits on the relay and answers each side herself.

Alice sends          : the meeting is at 4pm, bring the documents
MALLORY READS        : the meeting is at 4pm, bring the documents   <-- plaintext, fully readable
Mallory re-encrypts  : the meeting is CANCELLED, go home
Bob receives         : the meeting is CANCELLED, go home
Bob's AEAD tag check : VALID (he has no way to know)

RESULT: TOTAL COMPROMISE. Plain DH authenticates nothing. Alice
        proved she can do DH, not that she is Alice. Encryption was
        never broken - the attacker was simply handed the keys.

But note the limit: this only works if the humans actually check,
over a channel Mallory does not control. The maths cannot force that.

==========================================================================
EXPERIMENT E - what the relay still learns (metadata)
==========================================================================
plaintext                                                      wire len
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
hi                                                                   26
can you talk                                                         36
no                                                                   26
I am resigning on Monday and taking the client list with me          83

RESULT: ATTACK SUCCEEDS. The encrypted payload preserves length and
        the record adds a fixed 24 bytes, so the relay recovers the
        plaintext length exactly by subtracting 24. It also sees
        who talks to whom, when, and how often. A short reply after a
        long message is visibly a 'no'. My design does not defend this
        at all, and padding + cover traffic would be the fix.

SUMMARY: A, B, B2 and C defended. D and E are real, unfixed weaknesses.
